## Services and draft delivery targets

- **Release Readiness Review:** AI-assisted, owner-accountable static evidence review and one repository-specific plan, targeted within five business days after scope acceptance.
- **Release Repair:** one agreed outcome for a public repository, a focused branch or pull request, test evidence, notes, and one in-scope revision, targeted within ten business days.
- **Linux Launch Bundle:** up to two agreed outcomes, targeted within fifteen business days.

The written order controls scope and delivery timing. Upstream review or merge is outside the seller's control and is never guaranteed.

## Acceptance, cancellation, and refunds

- No order exists until the owner accepts the written scope, price, timing, exclusions, and repository commit, and the payment processor confirms payment.
- A customer may cancel for a full refund before work begins.
- After work begins, the order-specific terms must state the completed milestone value and any refundable remainder. If the seller cannot deliver the agreed artifact, the undelivered amount is refunded.
- Processor delays, fees, currency, taxes, chargeback handling, and any statutory cancellation/refund rights must be disclosed before checkout. Statutory rights override this draft.
- Scope changes require a new written agreement; there is no automatic recurring charge.

## Customer and upstream responsibilities

The customer confirms that the repository and requested work are public and that they are authorized to request the service. The customer identifies material contribution rules, contributor attestations, or third-party rights they know apply. A human signs any CLA, DCO, or personal certification.

Project patches follow the upstream repository's license and contribution terms. The service does not transfer rights the seller does not own.

## Privacy and retention

Launch scope uses public repository data only. The service does not accept private code, credentials, production access, or payment details outside the owner-controlled processor.

Collect only the public repository URL, requested outcome, reply route, agreement records, and legally required transaction records. Keep correspondence and processor/tax records outside the public project repository with least-privileged access. Delete non-public working service copies and non-required contact data within 30 days after delivery or cancellation; retain transaction and tax records only for the legally required period. A customer may request access, correction, or deletion through the support contact, subject to legal retention duties and the public-record limitation below.

Public case studies require separate affirmative permission. Service purchase never implies permission to publish a customer's identity, correspondence, or endorsement. Revocation stops new first-party promotional display or use where practical; it cannot erase an upstream pull request, public Git history, forks, caches, archives, or third-party copies. Public patches and reports may persist indefinitely, so the customer must approve the exact public artifact before publication.

## Limitations

The work is maintenance and release-engineering assistance, not a security audit, penetration test, legal or tax advice, certification, or guarantee of compatibility, package publication, upstream acceptance, revenue, or business results. Final warranty, liability, governing-law, dispute, and accessibility language requires owner/legal review before launch.
